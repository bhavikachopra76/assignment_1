0. DOCUMENT CONTROL
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.1 PURPOSE
This guide defines how performance is planned, measured, reviewed, rated and
rewarded at NexaCore Technologies, and how employees progress through grades
L1 to L6. It exists so that a review at the Northern Regional Hub in Patiala
follows the same standard as one at the Global Headquarters in Bengaluru, and
so that an employee can see in writing exactly what the next grade requires.
For employees it explains how work is assessed; for managers it sets the
standard for goals, evidence and calibration; for HR and Leadership it keeps
ratings consistent across 4,500 people and eight departments.

0.2 SCOPE
Covered: the annual cycle, the rating scale, goal setting, promotion, career
paths, the Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) and recognition. Not covered:
compensation terms (appointment letter); leave entitlement (Leave Policy);
disciplinary procedure (Code of Conduct); recruitment (Employee Handbook).
Performance management addresses capability and delivery. Conduct matters -
harassment, dishonesty, policy breach - are handled under the Code of Conduct
and are never routed through a PIP.

SECTION 1 - PERFORMANCE PHILOSOPHY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 WHY PERFORMANCE REVIEWS EXIST
NexaCore engineers document intelligence platforms for enterprise clients in
banking, insurance, healthcare, manufacturing and the public sector. That work
is long-cycle and collaborative, and its quality is not visible from output
volume alone. Reviews exist to make an honest, evidenced judgement about
contribution and convert it into three things the employee can use: clear
feedback, a development plan, and a fair share of reward and advancement. A
review is not a ranking exercise or a way of distributing a fixed pool of high
ratings. NexaCore operates no forced distribution curve; if a whole team
genuinely exceeds expectations, the whole team may be rated 4.

1.2 CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
The formal cycle has two review points, but performance management is
continuous. Managers hold a documented check-in with each team member at least
monthly, recorded in the HRMS. The purpose is to remove surprises: nothing
raised for the first time in a March review is fair, and a rating of 1 or 2
never discussed during the year reflects a failure of management as much as of
performance. Feedback must be specific, timely and behavioural - what was done
and what effect it had, not what the person is like.

1.3 CAREER GROWTH
Every employee sits on a defined ladder from L1 to L6, with a published
minimum tenure, certification expectation and salary band for each step
(Section 5.3). Growth is not a reward for waiting. It is granted when an
employee is already operating at the next grade and the business has a place
for them to do so.

1.4 BUSINESS ALIGNMENT
Individual goals cascade from department and company objectives so that
personal success and company success point the same way. A goal that cannot
be traced to a department objective is usually a task rather than a goal and
should be reconsidered at goal setting in April.

1.5 EMPLOYEE DEVELOPMENT
Development is funded, not merely encouraged. All employees from L1 to L6
receive enterprise access to Udemy for Business and LinkedIn Learning. The
annual certification budget is 25,000 rupees for L2 and L3 and 50,000 rupees
for L4 and above, subject to pre-approval by an L4 or above and relevance to
the role. Employees at L3 and above are eligible for one company-sponsored
domestic technology conference each year, approved by Rohan Gupta, VP of
Engineering. Unused development budget is a management shortfall, not a
saving.

SECTION 2 - PERFORMANCE REVIEW CYCLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 CYCLE OVERVIEW
NexaCore runs two formal review cycles each financial year, both administered
in the HRMS. The financial year runs 1 April to 31 March.

  CYCLE            MONTH     PURPOSE AND OUTCOME
  ---------------  --------  -------------------------------------------
  Mid-Year Review  October   Developmental focus. No compensation changes
                             in this cycle. Used to recalibrate goals,
                             identify development needs, course-correct.
  Annual Review    March     Performance ratings are locked. Influences
                             the April increment cycle and the annual
                             promotion cycle effective 1 April.

2.2 MID-YEAR REVIEW (OCTOBER)
The Mid-Year Review is developmental: no rating of record, no increment, no
promotion decision. It confirms that April goals remain relevant, resets goals
overtaken by changed client or programme priorities, gives the employee an
unambiguous read on where they stand, and agrees development actions with
enough of the year left to matter. Managers must give a directional indication
- below, at, or above expectations. Withholding it because the conversation is
uncomfortable is not acceptable: an employee heading for a 2 in March has a
right to know in October. Although this cycle makes no compensation decision,
variable pay is paid bi-annually in the October and April payroll cycles,
prorated on individual ratings; the October payment is driven by the previous
March rating, not by the Mid-Year Review.

2.3 ANNUAL REVIEW (MARCH)
The Annual Review is the rating of record. It determines the April increment,
promotion eligibility, and variable pay for the following year. Ratings are
locked at the end of March and cannot be altered afterwards except through
the correction route in Section 2.8.

2.4 SELF ASSESSMENT
Both cycles open with the employee's self assessment in the HRMS. The employee
reviews each goal, states the outcome achieved, and provides evidence -
verifiable artefacts such as delivery milestones met, defect and quality
metrics, client or stakeholder feedback, Confluence documentation, merged pull
requests in GitHub Enterprise, closed Jira items, certifications completed, or
colleagues mentored. It is not a negotiation position and should not be
inflated, since managers usually see the same evidence; nor understated, since
under-reporting makes it harder to advocate for you in calibration. A missing
self assessment does not stop the cycle; the manager rates on what is
available.

2.5 MANAGER REVIEW
The manager reviews the self assessment, adds their assessment against each
goal, gathers peer and stakeholder input, and proposes a rating on the 1 to 5
scale with recorded evidence. A proposed rating without recorded evidence is
returned by the HRBP. For employees at L3 and above, and for anyone working
primarily on a client engagement, managers must seek input from at least two
stakeholders outside their own reporting line.

2.6 CALIBRATION
Calibration keeps ratings comparable across teams, departments and the four
office locations; without it, a 4 from a generous manager in Pune would not
mean the same as a 4 from a demanding manager in Hyderabad. Sessions are
chaired by an L5 Director with the HRBP present and attended by all L4
managers in the group. Managers present proposed ratings with evidence, and
the panel challenges outliers in both directions - unexplained clusters of 4s
and 5s, and low ratings never raised at Mid-Year. Ratings may move up or down,
and the manager must be able to explain any change in their own words.
Calibration applies no forced distribution: it tests whether the evidence
supports the rating, using one standard for every employee at a grade.

2.7 FINAL RATINGS AND COMMUNICATION
After calibration, ratings are approved by the HRBP and locked in the HRMS at
the end of March. The manager communicates the rating in a one-to-one
conversation, never by email or chat alone, and records the discussion in the
HRMS. The employee then acknowledges it. Acknowledgement records that the
rating was received and discussed; it does not signify agreement.

2.8 DISAGREEING WITH A RATING
An employee may record a written disagreement in the HRMS within 10 working
days of communication. The HRBP reviews the rating, evidence and calibration
record and may uphold it or refer it back to the calibration chair; the
outcome is communicated within 15 working days. Where the employee believes
the rating reflects bias or retaliation rather than performance, the matter
escalates to the CHRO, Ritika Sharma, under the Code of Conduct grievance
route instead.

  April             Increments and promotions effective  HR / Payroll

SECTION 3 - PERFORMANCE RATING SCALE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 THE FIVE-POINT SCALE
The rating of record is set once a year, in the March Annual Review.

  RATING  LABEL                 CONSEQUENCE
  ------  --------------------  -----------------------------------------
  1       Unacceptable          Requires immediate PIP (Performance
                                Improvement Plan)
  2       Needs Improvement     Structured development plan; not
                                promotion eligible
  3       Meets Expectations    Standard rating; standard increment and
                                variable pay
  4       Exceeds Expectations  Promotion eligible
  5       Outstanding           Exceptional impact; highest increment and
                                variable pay

3.2 RATING 1 - UNACCEPTABLE
Performance falls materially and persistently below the requirements of the
grade. Commitments are missed without early escalation, work needs
substantial rework, and previous feedback has produced no change. A rating of
1 requires an immediate PIP, the 60-day plan described in Section 7.
  Example: An Associate (L2) in QA and Automation has over two quarters
  signed off test cycles that later failed in staging, missed three
  regression deadlines without flagging them, and not completed the mandatory
  Information Security refresher despite four reminders. The issues were
  raised at Mid-Year and in monthly check-ins with no measurable change. The
  manager rates 1 and initiates a PIP.

3.3 RATING 2 - NEEDS IMPROVEMENT
Performance falls short of the grade in one or more significant areas, but the
shortfall is specific and addressable rather than systemic. The employee
delivers, but with supervision, rework or delay not expected at their grade. A
rating of 2 does not trigger a PIP; it requires a documented development plan
with named actions, owners and review dates, revisited at the next Mid-Year.
An employee rated 2 is not promotion eligible and typically receives a
below-standard increment. Two consecutive 2s are reviewed by the manager and
HRBP to decide whether a PIP is warranted.
  Example: A Senior Executive (L3) in Backend Engineering delivers features to
  specification, but design documents consistently arrive after implementation
  has begun, and two production incidents traced to edge cases missed in his
  design review. Technical quality is adequate; the shortfall is the design
  rigour and documentation discipline expected at L3.

3.4 RATING 3 - MEETS EXPECTATIONS
The standard rating, and the correct rating for a solid, reliable performer.
The employee consistently delivers what the grade requires, to the expected
quality, timeline and independence; goals are substantially achieved and
feedback is acted on. A rating of 3 is not a criticism or a signal of
stagnation - most NexaCore employees are rated 3 in any year, and they receive
the standard increment and variable pay. They are not promotion eligible that
cycle, because promotion requires a minimum rating of 4.
  Example: An Associate (L2) in Frontend Engineering meets all sprint
  commitments, keeps code review turnaround within the team standard, responds
  constructively to feedback, and completed her AWS Developer certification
  within budget. She has not taken on work beyond assigned scope. Rating: 3.

3.5 RATING 4 - EXCEEDS EXPECTATIONS
The employee consistently delivers beyond the requirements of the grade -
typically operating at or near the next grade in one or more dimensions:
ownership beyond assigned scope, raising the performance of others, resolving
problems that were not theirs to resolve, or delivering measurable business
impact. Rating 4 is the minimum for promotion consideration, but a 4 alone
does not produce a promotion; the tenure, certification and approval
requirements in Section 5 must also be met.
  Example: A Senior Executive (L3) in DevOps and Site Reliability identifies
  that a client-facing service will breach its response-time commitment under
  projected month-end volume. Rather than wait for the breach he raises it
  with his L4 manager the same day, proposes two mitigations with effort
  estimates, documents the analysis on Confluence, and implements the chosen
    option before the peak. He also mentors two L2 associates through on-call.
  Rating: 4.

3.6 RATING 5 - OUTSTANDING
Exceptional impact well beyond the grade, visible at department or company
level rather than within the immediate team. Rating 5 is reserved for a
genuinely small number of employees each year and requires specific,
verifiable evidence presented in calibration.
  Example: A Manager (L4) in the Document Intelligence Team finds a recurring
  manual reconciliation step consuming several hours a week across three
  delivery pods. She designs and delivers an automated approach, documents it
  on Confluence, drives adoption across all three pods and a fourth in Pune,
  and the change is later productised into the platform. The saving is
  measurable, client escalations fall, and two of her team are promoted having
  been developed by her that year.

3.7 HOW RATINGS AFFECT REWARD
  RATING  INCREMENT         VARIABLE PAY      PROMOTION ELIGIBLE
  ------  ----------------  ----------------  ------------------------
  1       None              None              No - PIP initiated
  2       Below standard    Reduced           No
  3       Standard          Standard          No
  4       Above standard    Above standard    Yes, if Section 5 is met
  5       Highest           Highest           Yes, if Section 5 is met

Variable pay is paid bi-annually in the October and April payroll cycles,
prorated on the individual rating. Salary is credited on the last working day
of every month. Increments from the March rating take effect on 1 April.

SECTION 4 - GOAL SETTING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 WHEN GOALS ARE SET
Goals are set in April, at the start of the financial year, and locked in the
HRMS. They are reviewed at the October Mid-Year Review and may be reset where
client or programme priorities have changed, or amended mid-cycle by
agreement, with the change and reason recorded. An employee cannot be rated
against a goal that was never recorded.

4.2 SMART GOALS
Every goal must be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound.
  Specific    states exactly what will be delivered, not a direction of
              effort.
  Measurable  carries a number, a date or a verifiable artefact.
  Achievable  is realistic for the grade, capacity and dependencies.
  Relevant    traces to a department or company objective.
  Time-bound  has a date, not merely "this year".

  Weak goal   : "Improve code quality."
  SMART goal  : "Reduce escaped defects in the payments module from an
                 average of 7 per release to 3 or fewer by 31 December,
                 measured in Jira, by introducing contract tests and a
                 design review checklist."

4.3 KPIs AND OKRs
NexaCore uses both frameworks, for different purposes. They are
complementary, not alternatives.

  ASPECT      KPI                         OKR
  ----------  --------------------------  -----------------------------
  Purpose     Ongoing health of the role  A step change or new outcome
  Horizon     Continuous, reviewed        Quarterly
              quarterly
  Target      Expected to be met          Ambitious; 70 percent
                                          attainment is a good result
  Example     "Sprint commitment          "Reduce median extraction
              attainment at or above      latency from 900ms to 400ms
              85 percent"                 by end of Q3"
  Rating use  Consistent misses indicate  Judged on outcome and
              a 2 or below                learning, not on percentage

Because OKRs are deliberately ambitious, missing an OKR target is not by
itself evidence of underperformance. Managers judge the quality of the
attempt, the learning captured and the outcome delivered.

4.4 INDIVIDUAL AND TEAM GOALS
Every employee carries individual goals, measuring their own contribution, and
team or organisational goals, measuring what their group delivers. The balance
shifts with grade: an intern is judged almost entirely on their own work, a
Director largely on what their organisation delivers.

  GRADE   INDIVIDUAL GOALS   TEAM / ORGANISATIONAL GOALS
  ------  -----------------  ---------------------------
  L1      80 percent         20 percent
  L2      70 percent         30 percent
  L3      70 percent         30 percent
  L4      50 percent         50 percent
  L5      30 percent         70 percent
  L6      20 percent         80 percent

4.5 GOAL WEIGHTAGE
Each goal carries a weight, and all weights must total 100 percent. Employees
carry three to six goals: fewer usually means the role is under-described,
more dilutes focus. No single goal should exceed 40 percent, so one dependency
failure outside the employee's control cannot determine the whole rating.

  Worked example - Senior Executive (L3), Document Intelligence Team:
  GOAL                                                        WEIGHT
  ----------------------------------------------------------  ------
  Deliver the classification service upgrade to production
  by 30 September with zero Sev-1 defects in 30 days             35%
  Reduce median extraction latency from 900ms to 400ms by
  end of Q3 (OKR)                                                25%
  Maintain sprint commitment attainment at or above 85
  percent across the year (KPI)                                  15%
  Complete CKA certification within the annual budget by
  31 January                                                     15%
  Mentor two L2 associates through their first production
  release, evidenced in the HRMS mentorship record               10%
  ----------------------------------------------------------  ------
  TOTAL                                                         100%

SECTION 5 - PROMOTION PROCESS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 PROMOTION CYCLE
Promotions run on a single annual cycle, effective 1 April of every financial
year. There is no off-cycle promotion route: an employee who becomes eligible
in June waits for the following April.

5.2 ELIGIBILITY - THE THREE CONDITIONS
All three must be satisfied at the same time.
  1. TENURE. A minimum of 18 months in the current grade, subject to the
     grade-specific minimum tenure in Section 5.3.
  2. PERFORMANCE. A minimum annual rating of 4 - Exceeds Expectations in the
     March Annual Review.
  3. APPROVAL. A direct manager recommendation from L4 or above, plus HR
     Business Partner approval. Promotion to L6 additionally requires Board
     approval.
Meeting all three makes an employee eligible for consideration; it does not
create an entitlement. Promotion also requires that the business has a role at
the next grade and that the employee already demonstrates capability at it.

5.3 PROMOTION MATRIX, TENURE, CERTIFICATIONS AND SALARY BANDS
Salary bands are annual base salary in Indian Rupees.

  FROM   TO   MIN TENURE   CERTIFICATIONS (EXAMPLES)    BASE SALARY BAND
  -----  ---  -----------  ---------------------------  --------------------
  L1     L2   6 months     AWS Cloud Practitioner       6,00,000 - 9,00,000
  L2     L3   18 months    AWS Developer / CKA          9,00,000 - 15,00,000
  L3     L4   24 months    PMP / Scrum Master / CISSP   15,00,000 - 28,00,000
  L4     L5   36 months    Exec. Leadership Program     28,00,000 - 45,00,000
  L5     L6   Not appl.    Board Approval Required      45,00,000 and above

  GRADE  TITLE CLASSIFICATION           MANAGEMENT LEVEL
  -----  -----------------------------  ---------------------------------
  L1     Intern / Trainee               Individual Contributor
  L2     Associate / Junior Executive   Individual Contributor
  L3     Senior Executive / Specialist  Individual Contributor / Team Lead
  L4     Manager / Principal            Mid-Level Management
  L5     Director / VP                  Senior Management
  L6     C-Suite (CEO, CTO, CFO)        Executive Leadership

5.4 REQUIRED CERTIFICATIONS
Certifications must be recorded in the HRMS to count; a certificate held but
not uploaded does not count. Costs are reimbursable within the annual budget -
25,000 rupees for L2 and L3, 50,000 rupees for L4 and above - provided the
certification is pre-approved by an L4 or above and relevant to the role.
Certifications claimed without prior approval are not reimbursed, and claims
go in the HRMS within 30 days. The certifications named are examples of the
standard expected, not an exhaustive list; an equivalent of comparable rigour
may be accepted with HRBP agreement recorded in the HRMS.

5.6 SALARY REVISION ON PROMOTION
A promotion moves the employee into the base salary band of the new grade
(Section 5.3), effective 1 April with the annual increment cycle. Placement
within the band reflects experience, the calibrated rating and position
relative to peers at that grade. An employee already paid above the floor of
the new band still receives an increase; a promotion never results in a nil
revision. Provident Fund at 12 percent of basic salary, gratuity accrual and
insurance cover adjust automatically.

5.7 PROMOTION AND PROBATION
Full-Time Employees serve a six-month probation. Time on probation counts
towards grade tenure, but an employee must be confirmed before a promotion
recommendation can be submitted. Employees on probation are not rated on the
standard scale; they are assessed through the 30-day, 90-day and 150-day
probation check-ins described in the Employee Handbook.

5.8 INTERN CONVERSION (L1 TO L2)
Interns are not promoted through the standard cycle. Conversion from L1 to L2
requires a minimum of 6 months in the internship, a positive end-of-internship
assessment, an available L2 role, and HRBP approval. AWS Cloud Practitioner is
the example certification standard. Converted interns begin a fresh six-month
probation and enter the L2 salary band.

5.9 CROSS-REFERENCE
The promotion rules here restate those published in the Employee Handbook
(NCT-HR-HB-004, version 4.0), Section 3.10. Where any difference arises, the
Employee Handbook prevails.

SECTION 6 - CAREER GROWTH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 TWO CAREER PATHS
From grade L3 onwards NexaCore offers two parallel paths of equal standing.
An employee is not required to manage people in order to advance.

  GRADE  TECHNICAL / IC PATH             MANAGEMENT PATH
  -----  ------------------------------  -------------------------------
  L2     Associate                       Associate
  L3     Senior Executive / Specialist   Senior Executive / Team Lead
  L4     Principal                       Manager
  L5     Distinguished / VP (technical)  Director / VP
  L6     C-Suite (for example CTO)       C-Suite

Both paths carry the same grade, salary band and promotion requirements: a
Principal at L4 and a Manager at L4 are peers. Employees may move between
paths at a grade boundary with manager and HRBP agreement; moving from Manager
to
Principal is not a demotion and is not recorded as one.

6.2 WHAT CHANGES AS GRADE INCREASES
  L1  Learns the environment; delivers defined tasks with close guidance.
  L2  Delivers assigned work independently within an established pattern.
  L3  Owns a component or workstream end to end; sets technical direction
      within it; mentors L1 and L2 colleagues.
  L4  Owns a team or technical domain; accountable for outcome, not only
      output; develops others; approves domestic travel and expenses.
  L5  Owns a department or major programme; sets direction and budget;
      approves international travel; chairs calibration.
  L6  Owns company-level strategy and results.

6.3 LEARNING
All employees from L1 to L6 receive enterprise access to Udemy for Business
and LinkedIn Learning through the internal portal. Certification is funded
within the budget in Section 5.4. Employees at L3 and above are eligible for
one company-sponsored domestic technology conference each year, such as AWS
Summit or Google Cloud Next, approved by Rohan Gupta, VP of Engineering.
Attendees share their learning through a Confluence write-up or an internal
session. Full details are in the Employee Handbook, Section 8.

6.4 MENTORSHIP
Employees at L2 and L3 are matched with a mentor at L4 or above from outside
their reporting line, so mentoring stays separate from evaluation. Interns at
L1 get a mentor at L3 or L4 alongside their reporting manager, and every new
joiner is assigned a buddy on Day 1. Pairings are recorded in the HRMS and
reviewed every six months. Mentoring others is legitimate review evidence and
is frequently part of the case for promotion to L4.

6.5 LEADERSHIP PROGRAMMES
The Executive Leadership Program is the named development expectation for
promotion from L4 to L5. It is nominated by an L5 Director, approved by the
HRBP, and covers organisational leadership, financial literacy, strategic
planning and people development. A first-time managers programme is offered
to employees moving from L3 to L4 on the management path, covering goal
setting, evidence, calibration and difficult conversations.

6.6 INTERNAL MOBILITY AND INTERNAL JOB POSTING
NexaCore fills roles internally wherever possible. All open positions are
published on the Internal Job Posting board in the HRMS before or alongside
external advertising.

  IJP ELIGIBILITY REQUIREMENT   STANDARD
  ----------------------------  ----------------------------------------
  Probation                     Must be completed
  Time in current role          Minimum 12 months
  Performance rating            Meets Expectations (3) or above
  Confidentiality               Confidential until the interview stage,
                                when the current manager is informed

6.7 CROSS-FUNCTIONAL TRANSFERS
A cross-functional transfer moves an employee between departments or
functional groups - for example from QA and Automation into the AI Platform
and Machine Learning team - at the same grade. Transfers require agreement
from the releasing manager, the receiving manager and the HRBP, raised in the
HRMS. A same-grade transfer does not reset grade tenure for promotion, but the
receiving manager may require a settling period of up to three months before
submitting a recommendation. Goals are reset on transfer, and the year's
rating is agreed jointly by both managers, weighted by time in each role.
Where the transfer involves company-requested relocation of more than 100
kilometres, the Expense and Reimbursement Policy relocation limits apply.

SECTION 7 - PERFORMANCE IMPROVEMENT PLAN (PIP)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 WHEN A PIP STARTS
A PIP is initiated when an employee receives a rating of 1 - Unacceptable in
the Annual Review. It may also be initiated mid-cycle where performance has
deteriorated materially and documented feedback has produced no improvement;
mid-cycle initiation requires HRBP approval. A PIP is a performance instrument
only - conduct matters go through the Code of Conduct, never a PIP.

7.2 DURATION
A PIP runs for 60 days from the date it is signed and acknowledged in the
HRMS. The duration is fixed. It may be paused only for approved long leave -
for example certified sick leave or maternity leave - and resumes for the
remaining days on return.

7.3 WHAT A PIP MUST CONTAIN
  - The specific performance gaps, described behaviourally with dated
    examples and reference to previous feedback.
  - Measurable improvement objectives with defined success criteria.
  - The support the company will provide: training, mentoring, reallocation
    of work, or closer supervision.
  - A weekly review schedule with dates.
  - A clear statement of the consequence of not meeting the objectives.
A PIP that lists complaints without measurable objectives and defined support
is invalid and will be returned by the HRBP.

7.4 MANAGER RESPONSIBILITIES
The manager drafts the PIP with the HRBP, holds the initial conversation in
person, meets the employee weekly, documents each review in the HRMS, provides
the promised support, and gives an honest interim indication at the 30-day
midpoint. A manager who does not hold the weekly reviews cannot rely on the
outcome; incomplete documentation invalidates the process.

7.5 EMPLOYEE EXPECTATIONS
The employee acknowledges the PIP in the HRMS, attends every weekly review,
works to meet the objectives, asks for clarification or support early, and
raises obstacles promptly. Employees may bring their HRBP into any PIP
discussion. A PIP is confidential between the employee, the manager, the HRBP
and the approving L5.

7.6 SUCCESSFUL COMPLETION
Where the objectives are met, the PIP is closed successfully in the HRMS and
the employee returns to normal performance management. A closed PIP does not
carry forward as a permanent mark: the employee is rated normally next year
and can be rated 3, 4 or 5 on that year's evidence. It does affect promotion
timing - a rating of 4 or above in a subsequent Annual Review is required
before a promotion recommendation can be submitted.

7.7 FAILURE OUTCOMES
Where the objectives are not met, the manager and HRBP review the evidence and
one of three outcomes follows: an extension of up to 30 days where there is
clear partial progress; redeployment at the same grade where the mismatch is
role fit rather than capability; or separation, with the applicable notice
period served - 60 days for Full-Time Employees at L2 and above. Separation
requires HRBP approval and L5 endorsement.

SECTION 8 - RECOGNITION PROGRAMMES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 PURPOSE AND PROGRAMME SUMMARY
Formal ratings occur once a year, but recognition should not wait for March.
NexaCore operates recognition programmes so contribution is acknowledged
close to the point at which it happens.

  PROGRAMME                  FREQUENCY   NOMINATED BY     APPROVED BY
  -------------------------  ----------  ---------------  --------------
  Spot Award                 Any time    Any employee     L4 Manager
  Employee of the Month      Monthly     Peers/managers   L4 + HRBP
  Innovation Award           Quarterly   Manager or peer  L5 Director
  Customer Excellence Award  Quarterly   Customer Success L5 Director
                                         or the client
  Long Service Award         Milestone   Automatic(HRMS)  HR
  Annual Recognition         Annual      L4 and above     L6 Leadership

8.2 SPOT AWARDS
Spot Awards recognise a specific act close to the moment: covering for a
colleague at short notice, an out-of-hours recovery, or documentation that
saved others time. Any employee may nominate any other, across departments and
grades, in the HRMS, approved by an L4 manager. They carry a modest monetary
value paid through payroll and are deliberately quick to award.

8.3 EMPLOYEE OF THE MONTH
Awarded monthly in each functional group on peer and manager nominations,
approved jointly by the L4 manager and the HRBP. The citation names the
specific contribution. Employees at L1 and C1 are eligible for nomination
alongside Full-Time Employees.

8.4 INNOVATION AWARDS
Awarded quarterly for a demonstrable improvement in how NexaCore builds or
operates - an automation that removes recurring manual effort, a reusable
platform capability, or a measurable performance gain. Evidence of adoption
beyond the originating team is expected. Approved by an L5 Director. Reflects
the Agile Innovation value.

8.5 CUSTOMER EXCELLENCE AWARD
Awarded quarterly for outstanding client outcomes, nominated by the Customer
Success team or directly by a client and approved by an L5 Director.
Nominations must cite the client, the situation and the outcome. This
programme reflects the Customer Centricity value.

8.6 LONG SERVICE AWARDS
Generated automatically by the HRMS at the 3, 5, 10 and 15-year milestones of
continuous service. The 5-year milestone is also the point at which gratuity
becomes payable, calculated at 15 days of basic salary for every completed
year of service.

8.7 ANNUAL RECOGNITION
Held after the March cycle closes, recognising company-level contribution
across engineering excellence, delivery, client impact, people leadership and
living the NexaCore values. Nominated by managers at L4 and above, approved by
L6 Leadership. Recognition is separate from ratings and variable pay: an award
does not by itself change a rating, and a rating of 3 does not disqualify an
employee from any programme.

SECTION 9 - FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1. When am I eligible for promotion?
When all three conditions are met at once: a minimum of 18 months in your
current grade, a minimum annual rating of 4 - Exceeds Expectations, and a
manager recommendation from L4 or above plus HRBP approval. Grade-specific
minimum tenure is in Section 5.3.

Q2. How is my performance measured?
Against the goals locked in the HRMS in April, using evidence. Individual and
team goals are weighted by grade (Section 4.4), each goal carries a weight
totalling 100 percent, and the rating is set in the March Annual Review after
calibration.

Q3. Can I disagree with my rating?
Yes. Record a written disagreement in the HRMS within 10 working days. Your
HRBP reviews the rating, evidence and calibration record and responds within
15 working days. If you believe the rating reflects bias or retaliation,
escalate to the CHRO, Ritika Sharma, under the Code of Conduct instead.

Q4. Who approves promotions?
Your direct manager (L4 or above) recommends and the HRBP approves. Promotion
to L5 requires L6 leadership approval; promotion to L6 requires Board
approval.

Q5. How long must I stay in my current grade before promotion?
6 months from L1 to L2, 18 months from L2 to L3, 24 months from L3 to L4, and
36 months from L4 to L5. Promotion to L6 has no fixed tenure and requires
Board approval.

Q6. What certifications help with promotion?
The published examples are AWS Cloud Practitioner (L1 to L2); AWS Developer
or CKA (L2 to L3); PMP, Scrum Master or CISSP (L3 to L4); and the Executive
Leadership Program (L4 to L5). It must be recorded in the HRMS to count.

Q7. Will the company pay for my certification?
Yes, within the annual budget - 25,000 rupees for L2 and L3, 50,000 rupees
for L4 and above - provided it is pre-approved by an L4 or above and relevant
to your role. Claims go in the HRMS within 30 days of the expense.

Q8. Can interns receive promotions?
Not through the standard cycle. Conversion from L1 to L2 requires a minimum
of 6 months in the internship, a positive end-of-internship assessment, an
available L2 role and HRBP approval (Section 5.8).

Q9. Are contractors rated and promoted?
No. Contractors at C1 are managed by the engagement owner under their
contract of engagement. They are not rated on the NexaCore scale and are not
promotion eligible.

Q10. How do salary revisions work?
Increments from the March rating take effect on 1 April. On promotion you
move into the base salary band of your new grade (Section 5.3), also
effective 1 April. A promotion never results in a nil revision.

Q11. When is variable pay paid?
Bi-annually, in the October and April payroll cycles, prorated on your
individual rating. The October payment is driven by the previous March
rating, not by the Mid-Year Review.

Q12. I was rated 3. Am I underperforming?
No. Rating 3 - Meets Expectations is the standard rating for a solid,
reliable performer, and most employees are rated 3 in a given year. It does
mean you are not promotion eligible that cycle, since promotion requires a 4.

Q13. Does NexaCore use a forced distribution or bell curve?
No. Calibration tests whether the evidence supports the proposed rating,
using the same standard for everyone at a grade. If a whole team genuinely
exceeds expectations, the whole team may be rated 4.

Q14. What happens if I am placed on a PIP?
It runs 60 days from signature, with weekly documented reviews and a 30-day
interim indication. If you meet the objectives it closes successfully and
does not carry forward as a permanent mark, though you will need a rating of
4 or above in a later Annual Review before a promotion recommendation.

Q15. What happens if I do not meet my PIP objectives?
One of three outcomes: an extension of up to 30 days where there is clear
partial progress; redeployment at the same grade where the issue is role fit;
or separation with the applicable notice period, which is 60 days for
Full-Time Employees at L2 and above. Separation requires HRBP approval and L5
endorsement.

Q16. Can I change departments?
Yes, through the Internal Job Posting board in the HRMS. You must have
completed probation, spent at least 12 months in your current role, and hold
a rating of Meets Expectations (3) or above. Applications are confidential
until the interview stage.

Q17. Do I have to become a manager to progress?
No. From L3 onwards the technical and management paths run in parallel with
the same grades, salary bands and promotion requirements. A Principal at L4
and a Manager at L4 are peers, and moving between paths is not a demotion.

Q18. How does long leave affect my rating?
It does not disadvantage you. Your rating is based on the period actually
worked, and absence on approved leave - including the 26 weeks of maternity
leave under the Leave Policy - is never a rating factor. A PIP in progress is
paused for approved long leave and resumes on return.

Q19. What if my goals became irrelevant during the year?
Raise it with your manager. Goals may be reset at the October Mid-Year Review
or amended by agreement at any time, with the change and reason recorded in
the HRMS. You cannot be rated against a goal that was never recorded.

Q20. My manager never raised a concern until March. Is that acceptable?
No. Managers must hold documented monthly check-ins and give a directional
indication at Mid-Year. A low rating raised for the first time in March is a
management failure. Say so in your written disagreement under Section 2.8 and
raise it with your HR Business Partner.
